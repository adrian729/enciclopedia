## 1. Zustandspassiv — State Passive

In Week 7 you met the **Vorgangspassiv** (process passive): *Das Fenster **wird** geöffnet* — it reports an action in progress. This week you learn its sibling, the **Zustandspassiv** (state passive): *Das Fenster **ist** geöffnet* — it reports the result, the state that exists after the action. Where the Vorgangspassiv answers "what is happening to X?", the Zustandspassiv answers "in what state is X?". The distinction is the single most important passive idea at B2, because German uses both forms side by side every day.

### 1.1. Formation: sein + Partizip II

The Zustandspassiv is built exactly like a sein sentence: **sein (conjugated) + Partizip II at the end**. The verb werden does not appear — that is the whole contrast with the Vorgangspassiv. The subject of a Zustandspassiv is the object of the action, and the doer is almost never named, because the point is the state, not the action.

**3 formation examples:**

1. Die Tür **ist geschlossen**. (The door is closed.)
2. Der Brief **ist geschrieben**. (The letter is written.)
3. Das Problem **ist gelöst**. (The problem is solved.)

> **Trap (critical):** the Zustandspassiv looks identical to the Perfekt of a sein-verb (*Er ist gegangen*), but it is not. The test is *worden*: *Die Tür ist geöffnet **worden*** is Vorgangspassiv in Perfekt (the action took place); *Die Tür ist geöffnet* alone is Zustandspassiv (the state exists now).

### 1.2. Vorgangspassiv vs. Zustandspassiv: 5 Contrastive Pairs

**5 contrastive pairs:**

| Vorgangspassiv (action) | Zustandspassiv (state) | English |
|-------------------------|-------------------------|---------|
| Das Fenster **wird** geöffnet. | Das Fenster **ist** geöffnet. | The window is being opened. / The window is open. |
| Die Tür **wird** geschlossen. | Die Tür **ist** geschlossen. | The door is being closed. / The door is closed. |
| Der Brief **wird** geschrieben. | Der Brief **ist** geschrieben. | The letter is being written. / The letter is written. |
| Der Kuchen **wird** gebacken. | Der Kuchen **ist** gebacken. | The cake is being baked. / The cake is baked. |
| Das Licht **wird** ausgeschaltet. | Das Licht **ist** ausgeschaltet. | The light is being switched off. / The light is off. |

> **Decision test:** ask yourself "is something happening right now?" — that is Vorgangspassiv; or "has the action been completed and a state exists?" — that is Zustandspassiv. *Die Tür wird geschlossen* means someone is closing it at this moment; *Die Tür ist geschlossen* means it was closed before and remains closed.

### 1.3. Zustandspassiv Across Tenses: Chart and 3 Examples

The Zustandspassiv can be conjugated in every tense by putting sein into the right tense. Note the Futur carefully: **wird + Partizip II + sein**.

**Chart across tenses (das Fenster, geöffnet):**

| Tense | Form | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| Präsens | ist + geöffnet | Das Fenster **ist geöffnet**. |
| Präteritum | war + geöffnet | Das Fenster **war geöffnet**. |
| Perfekt | ist + geöffnet gewesen | Das Fenster **ist geöffnet gewesen**. |
| Plusquamperfekt | war + geöffnet gewesen | Das Fenster **war geöffnet gewesen**. |
| Futur I | wird + geöffnet sein | Das Fenster **wird geöffnet sein**. |

**3 examples:**

1. Die Bibliothek **ist** heute **geschlossen**. (The library is closed today. — Präsens)
2. Als wir ankamen, **war** die Tür schon **geschlossen**. (When we arrived, the door was already closed. — Präteritum)
3. Morgen um neun **wird** das Tor **geöffnet sein**. (Tomorrow at nine the gate will be open. — Futur)

> **Futur trap:** the Futur of the Zustandspassiv stacks **werden + sein**: *wird geöffnet sein*. Never write ~~wird geöffnet werden sein~~ — that mixes the Vorgangspassiv into a Zustandspassiv future and is wrong.

## 2. Advanced Passive Constructions

The plain Vorgangspassiv (Week 7) is only the beginning. This section covers the passive with modal verbs and, more importantly, the three **passive alternatives** that German speakers actually use in conversation and writing: **man + active**, **sich lassen + Infinitiv**, and **sein + zu + Infinitiv**. A native speaker reaches for these constantly — the passive proper is comparatively rare in everyday speech.

### 2.1. Passive with Modal Verbs: 6 Examples, One per Modal

Formation: **conjugated modal in position 2 + Partizip II + werden as an infinitive at the very end.** The word order in main clauses is exactly the modal frame from Week 2, with the passive marker werden stuck at the end.

**6 examples — one per modal:**

1. **können:** Die Aufgabe **kann** heute noch **gelöst werden**. (The task can still be solved today.)
2. **müssen:** Die Formulare **müssen** bis Freitag **ausgefüllt werden**. (The forms must be filled out by Friday.)
3. **sollen:** Der Termin **soll** verschoben **werden**. (The appointment is supposed to be postponed.)
4. **dürfen:** Hier **darf** nicht geraucht **werden**. (Smoking is not permitted here.)
5. **wollen:** Die neue Regel **will** gründlich **geprüft werden**. (The new rule is to be examined thoroughly. — unusual but grammatical)
6. **mögen:** Das Gemälde **mag** von vielen **bewundert werden**. (The painting is admired by many. — rare, literary)

> **Trap (critical):** the passive marker is **werden as an infinitive at the very end** — never ~~geworden~~, never *Das Buch wird gelesen müssen*. In the Perfekt the double-infinitive rule from Week 8 applies: *Das Buch **hat** gelesen **werden müssen**.* (The book had to be read.)

### 2.2. Passive Alternative: man + Active (4 Pairs)

The most common alternative to the passive is **man + active verb**. man replaces the unnamed doer, and the verb stays active. In spoken German this is the default choice whenever the doer is unknown or unimportant.

**4 pairs:**

1. **Man spricht** hier Deutsch. — Hier **wird** Deutsch **gesprochen**. (German is spoken here.)
2. **Man repariert** das Auto in der Werkstatt. — Das Auto **wird** in der Werkstatt **repariert**. (The car is repaired in the workshop.)
3. **Man verkauft** die Karten an der Kasse. — Die Karten **werden** an der Kasse **verkauft**. (The tickets are sold at the box office.)
4. **Man hat** das Problem **gelöst**. — Das Problem **ist** gelöst **worden**. (The problem has been solved.)

> **Register rule:** man + active is what Germans actually say; the passive is more formal and written. If no agent is named and you want to sound natural in conversation, choose man. The passive sounds official, like a sign, a rule, or a report.

### 2.3. Passive Alternative: sich lassen + Infinitiv (4 Pairs)

**sich lassen + Infinitiv** expresses possibility and is exactly equivalent to **können + passive**. The form is active, but the meaning is passive: the subject does not act, it undergoes. In speech it is far more idiomatic than können + passive.

**4 pairs:**

1. Das **lässt sich reparieren**. — Das **kann repariert werden**. (That can be repaired.)
2. Das **lässt sich nicht vermeiden**. — Das **kann nicht vermieden werden**. (That cannot be avoided.)
3. Die Tür **lässt sich** leicht **öffnen**. — Die Tür **kann** leicht **geöffnet werden**. (The door can be opened easily.)
4. Die Fehler **lassen sich** kaum **beheben**. — Die Fehler **können** kaum **behoben werden**. (The errors can hardly be fixed.)

> **Word order:** the frame is **sich + lassen (position 2) + ... + Infinitiv (end)**: *Das lässt sich reparieren*. With **nicht** or **kaum** the construction means impossibility — *Das lässt sich nicht machen* (That cannot be done) — and this negative use is extremely common in everyday German.

### 2.4. Passive Alternative: sein + zu + Infinitiv (4 Pairs)

**sein + zu + Infinitiv** is the formal alternative to the passive with können or müssen. The context decides which one: with adverbs like leicht, schwer, kaum it means possibility; with rules and deadlines it means obligation.

**4 pairs:**

1. Das **ist zu reparieren**. — Das **kann repariert werden**. (That can be repaired.)
2. Die Aufgabe **ist leicht zu lösen**. — Die Aufgabe **kann leicht gelöst werden**. (The task is easy to solve.)
3. Die Regeln **sind zu beachten**. — Die Regeln **müssen beachtet werden**. (The rules must be observed.)
4. Der Brief **ist** noch nicht **zu unterschreiben**. — Der Brief **darf** noch nicht **unterschrieben werden**. (The letter is not to be signed yet.)

> **Meaning test:** *Das ist leicht zu merken* (can easily be remembered) = possibility; *Die Regeln sind zu beachten* (must be observed) = obligation. sein + zu is written and formal — you will meet it in signs, regulations, and academic prose, rarely in casual talk.

### 2.5. Choosing the Right Construction: A Decision Guide

**The decision guide:**

| Situation | Construction | Example |
|-----------|--------------|---------|
| The doer is named or important | Vorgangspassiv + von + Dativ | Das Bild wird von dem Künstler gemalt. |
| The result state matters, not the action | Zustandspassiv (sein + Partizip II) | Die Tür ist geschlossen. |
| A modal verb is needed | Modal + Partizip II + werden | Das muss gemacht werden. |
| Casual speech, no doer named | man + active | Man macht das so. |
| Possibility, "it works out somehow" | sich lassen + Infinitiv | Das lässt sich machen. |
| Formal text, possibility or obligation | sein + zu + Infinitiv | Das ist zu machen. |

**4 context-based exercises** (choose the construction, then compare with the answer key in §8.8):

1. You leave the house and want to tell a friend that the door is locked. Which construction fits?
2. A visitor asks where German is spoken here; you answer casually. Which construction fits?
3. You write a formal notice: the form must be completed. Which construction fits?
4. A customer worries that a scratch cannot be repaired; you reassure him that it is easy. Which construction fits?

> **Rule of thumb:** start from the situation, not from the passive. In speech prefer man and sich lassen; in writing prefer the passive proper, sein + zu, and extended participial phrases (§4). The Zustandspassiv is only for genuine states.

## 3. Participial Adjectives

Both participles can work as adjectives, and this is one of the most productive word-formation patterns in German. **Partizip I** (Infinitiv + d) describes an action that is active and ongoing; **Partizip II** describes an action that is completed and, for transitive verbs, passive. The participles decline exactly like ordinary adjectives (Week 3).

### 3.1. Partizip I as Adjective: Infinitiv + d + Ending

Formation: **Infinitiv + d + adjective ending** — schlafen → schlaf**end** → *das schlaf**ende** Kind*. The meaning is always **active/ongoing**: "a child that is (was) sleeping".

**6 examples:**

1. **schlafend** → das **schlafende** Kind (the sleeping child)
2. **lesend** → der **lesende** Mann (the reading man)
3. **singend** → die **singende** Frau (the singing woman)
4. **lächelnd** → das **lächelnde** Mädchen (the smiling girl)
5. **laufend** → der **laufende** Sportler (the running athlete)
6. **arbeitend** → die **arbeitenden** Menschen (the working people)

> **Accusative extension:** a Partizip I built from a transitive verb keeps its object: **die Zeitung lesende** Frau (the woman reading the newspaper). The object stands between the article and the participle — this is exactly the structure that §4 builds on.

### 3.2. Partizip II as Adjective: Partizip II + Ending

Formation: **Partizip II + adjective ending** — geschrieben → *das geschrieb**ene** Buch*. For transitive verbs the meaning is **passive/completed**: "the book that has been written". This is the adjective version of the Zustandspassiv.

**6 examples:**

1. **geschrieben** → das **geschriebene** Buch (the written book)
2. **gekocht** → das **gekochte** Wasser (the boiled water)
3. **gemalt** → das **gemalte** Bild (the painted picture)
4. **geöffnet** → das **geöffnete** Fenster (the open window)
5. **vergessen** → der **vergessene** Termin (the forgotten appointment)
6. **gewonnen** → das **gewonnene** Spiel (the won game)

### 3.3. Partizip I = Active/Ongoing vs. Partizip II = Passive/Completed

**4 contrastive pairs:**

1. der **lesende** Student (the student who is reading — active) vs. das **gelesene** Buch (the book that has been read — passive)
2. der **schreibende** Autor (the author who is writing — active) vs. der **geschriebene** Brief (the letter that has been written — passive)
3. das **brennende** Haus (the house that is burning — active) vs. das **verbrannte** Papier (the burnt paper — passive)
4. die **ankommenden** Gäste (the guests who are arriving — active) vs. die **angekommenen** Gäste (the guests who have arrived — completed)

> **Intransitive exception:** with intransitive verbs that form their Perfekt with sein, Partizip II is **active and completed**, never passive: *die angekommene Post* (the mail that has arrived), *der gestorbene Schriftsteller* (the writer who has died). Passive meaning arises only from transitive verbs.

## 4. Extended Participial Constructions (Erweiterte Partizipialattribute)

Formal written German has a way to pack a whole relative clause into a noun phrase: the **extended participial attribute** (das erweiterte Partizipialattribut). Instead of *der Mann, **der gestern ankam*** you read *der **gestern angekommene** Mann*. You will meet these constantly in newspapers, academic texts, and official documents — and you will hear them almost never, because they belong to writing.

### 4.1. Structure: Article + Modifiers + Partizip + Ending + Noun

The formula is **Article + all modifiers + Partizip (with adjective ending) + Noun**. The participle stands immediately before the noun; every modifier — adverbs, prepositional phrases, objects — stands between the article and the participle. Nothing may follow the noun except the verb of the clause.

**5 structure examples:**

1. der **gelesene** Roman (the read novel)
2. der **gestern gelesene** Roman (the novel read yesterday)
3. der **gestern von mir gelesene** Roman (the novel read yesterday by me)
4. das **auf dem Tisch liegende** Buch (the book lying on the table)
5. die **seit Jahren geplante** Reise (the trip planned for years)

> **Trap (critical):** all modifiers stay **between article and participle** — the construction is left-branching. ~~der gelesene gestern Roman~~ is impossible; the adverb must precede the participle: *der **gestern gelesene** Roman*. Never copy a relative-clause order into the participle phrase.

### 4.2. Left-Branching: 6 Examples of Increasing Complexity

**6 examples, from simple to fully extended:**

1. der **geschriebene** Brief (the written letter)
2. der **gestern geschriebene** Brief (the letter written yesterday)
3. der **gestern von mir geschriebene** Brief (the letter written by me yesterday)
4. der **gestern von mir mit großer Sorgfalt geschriebene** Brief (the letter written by me yesterday with great care)
5. das **vor hundert Jahren erbaute, kürzlich renovierte** Rathaus (the town hall built a hundred years ago and recently renovated)
6. die **von der Regierung vorgeschlagene und vom Parlament beschlossene** Reform (the reform proposed by the government and passed by parliament)

> **Reading strategy:** find the noun first, then the participle directly before it, then unpack the modifiers from right to left. In example 4: Brief (letter) ← geschriebene (written) ← von mir (by me) ← gestern (yesterday) ← mit großer Sorgfalt (with great care). You will never hear such a chain in conversation — it is a written phenomenon.

### 4.3. Relative Clause → Extended Participial Phrase (5 Pairs)

Convert each relative clause into an extended participial phrase. The tense and voice of the relative verb decide the participle: present active → Partizip I, passive → Partizip II.

**5 pairs:**

1. Der Mann, **der gestern ankam**, ist mein Onkel. — Der **gestern angekommene** Mann ist mein Onkel. (The man who arrived yesterday is my uncle.)
2. Das Kind, **das im Garten spielt**, ist laut. — Das **im Garten spielende** Kind ist laut. (The child playing in the garden is loud.)
3. Der Brief, **der gestern geschrieben wurde**, ist fertig. — Der **gestern geschriebene** Brief ist fertig. (The letter written yesterday is finished.)
4. Die Stadt, **die im Krieg zerstört wurde**, wurde wieder aufgebaut. — Die **im Krieg zerstörte** Stadt wurde wieder aufgebaut. (The city destroyed in the war was rebuilt.)
5. Die Probleme, **die im Bericht erwähnt werden**, sind gravierend. — Die **im Bericht erwähnten** Probleme sind gravierend. (The problems mentioned in the report are serious.)

### 4.4. Extended Participial Phrase → Relative Clause (5 Pairs)

Convert each extended participial phrase back into a relative clause. Partizip I becomes an active clause in the same tense; Partizip II becomes a passive clause.

**5 pairs:**

1. Der **gestern angekommene** Mann ist mein Onkel. — Der Mann, **der gestern ankam**, ist mein Onkel. (The man who arrived yesterday is my uncle.)
2. Das **im Garten spielende** Kind ist laut. — Das Kind, **das im Garten spielt**, ist laut. (The child playing in the garden is loud.)
3. Die **im Krieg zerstörte** Stadt wurde wieder aufgebaut. — Die Stadt, **die im Krieg zerstört wurde**, wurde wieder aufgebaut. (The city destroyed in the war was rebuilt.)
4. Die **von der Forschung entwickelte** Methode ist neu. — Die Methode, **die von der Forschung entwickelt wurde**, ist neu. (The method developed by the researchers is new.)
5. Der **die Zeitung lesende** Student sitzt am Fenster. — Der Student, **der die Zeitung liest**, sitzt am Fenster. (The student reading the newspaper is sitting by the window.)

### 4.5. When to Use Which: Formal vs. Spoken

**Formal/written → participial phrase; spoken → relative clause.** The participial phrase compresses information and sounds academic; the relative clause spells everything out and is easier to process on the fly.

**4 context examples:**

1. Written (newspaper): Die **gestern von der Regierung veröffentlichten** Zahlen überraschen. (The figures published yesterday by the government are surprising.)
2. Spoken: Die Zahlen, **die gestern von der Regierung veröffentlicht wurden**, überraschen. (same meaning, spoken)
3. Written (report): Der **seit Monaten geplante** Umbau beginnt. (The renovation planned for months is beginning.)
4. Spoken: Der Umbau, **der seit Monaten geplant wurde**, beginnt. (same meaning, spoken)

> **Rule of thumb:** in conversation use relative clauses almost always; in formal writing prefer participial phrases. If the chain of modifiers becomes too long to follow, break it into a relative clause even in writing — clarity always wins over density.

## 5. Konjunktiv II — Past (Irreale Vergangenheit)

In Week 7 you learned Konjunktiv II for unreal present situations (*Wenn ich Zeit hätte, ...*). This week you learn the past form, called **Irreale Vergangenheit** (unreal past): counterfactuals about things that did not happen — the German equivalent of "would have", "could have", "should have". This is the tense of regret, of hindsight, and of polite reproach.

### 5.1. Formation: hätte/wäre + Partizip II

Formation: **hätte or wäre (Konjunktiv II of haben/sein) + Partizip II at the end.** The choice between hätte and wäre follows exactly the Perfekt auxiliary rule from Week 2: verbs with haben in the Perfekt take hätte; movement and change-of-state verbs take wäre.

**Chart — Perfekt vs. Konjunktiv II Past:**

| Verb | Perfekt (indicative) | Konjunktiv II Past |
|------|----------------------|--------------------|
| haben | ich habe gehabt | ich **hätte gehabt** |
| sein | ich bin gewesen | ich **wäre gewesen** |
| gehen | ich bin gegangen | ich **wäre gegangen** |
| machen | ich habe gemacht | ich **hätte gemacht** |
| lesen | ich habe gelesen | ich **hätte gelesen** |
| kommen | ich bin gekommen | ich **wäre gekommen** |

> **Trap (critical):** the auxiliary must be **hätte/wäre**, never the indicative hatte/war. *Wenn ich Zeit **hatte**, kam ich* is real past; *Wenn ich Zeit **gehabt hätte**, wäre ich gekommen* is counterfactual. And note the position: in a wenn-clause the hätte/wäre stands **at the very end** — *... wenn ich Zeit gehabt **hätte**.*

### 5.2. Past Counterfactual Conditions (Type 3): 6 Examples

Type 3 = past condition + past result, both unreal. The wenn-clause ends with hätte/wäre; the main clause also uses hätte/wäre + Partizip II (never würde + infinitive for past events).

**6 examples:**

1. Wenn ich Zeit **gehabt hätte**, **hätte** ich dich **besucht**. (If I had had time, I would have visited you.)
2. Wenn wir früher **losgefahren wären**, **wären** wir pünktlich **gekommen**. (If we had left earlier, we would have arrived on time.)
3. Wenn er mehr **gelernt hätte**, **hätte** er die Prüfung **bestanden**. (If he had studied more, he would have passed the exam.)
4. Wenn das Wetter schön **gewesen wäre**, **wären** wir im Park **geblieben**. (If the weather had been nice, we would have stayed in the park.)
5. Wenn sie den Zug nicht **verpasst hätte**, **wäre** sie pünktlich **angekommen**. (If she had not missed the train, she would have arrived on time.)
6. Wenn ich das **gewusst hätte**, **hätte** ich anders **gehandelt**. (If I had known that, I would have acted differently.)

> **Pattern:** both clauses are verb-final structures built on hätte/wäre + Partizip II. The main clause is a normal V2 sentence: **Hätte ich dich besucht.** In the wenn-clause the auxiliary moves to the end: *... wenn ich Zeit gehabt **hätte**.*

### 5.3. Past Wishes: 4 Examples

Wishes about the past are Type 3 sentences in a wish form: **Hätte/Wäre ... doch/nur ... !** or **Wenn ... doch/nur ... hätte/wäre!** The particles doch and nur make the wish plaintive — "if only ...".

**4 examples:**

1. **Hätte** ich doch mehr **geübt**! / Wenn ich doch mehr **geübt hätte**! (If only I had practiced more!)
2. **Wäre** ich doch früher **gegangen**! (If only I had left earlier!)
3. **Hätte** ich das nur nicht **gesagt**! (If only I had not said that!)
4. **Hätte** ich damals auf meinen Vater **gehört**! (If only I had listened to my father back then!)

> **Emotion markers:** doch and nur carry the regret; the inverted form (*Hätte ich doch ...*) is more emotional than the wenn-form. The word order is V2 with the verb first: **Hätte ich doch mehr geübt.**

### 5.4. All Three Conditional Types Side by Side (3 Triplets)

You now know all three conditional types: **Type 0** (general truth, indicative), **Type 2** (present unreal, Konjunktiv II present), and **Type 3** (past unreal, Konjunktiv II past). Here they are side by side on three different topics.

**Triplet 1 — learning German:**

| Type | Sentence | English |
|------|----------|---------|

| Type 2 | Wenn ich mehr Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich mehr üben. | If I had more time, I would practice more. |
| Type 3 | Wenn ich mehr **geübt hätte**, **hätte** ich die Prüfung **bestanden**. | If I had practiced more, I would have passed the exam. |

**Triplet 2 — travel:**

| Type | Sentence | English |
|------|----------|---------|
| Type 0 | Wenn es regnet, **bleiben** wir zu Hause. | If it rains, we stay home. |
| Type 2 | Wenn ich Geld **hätte**, **würde** ich verreisen. | If I had money, I would travel. |
| Type 3 | Wenn ich Geld **gehabt hätte**, **wäre** ich verreist. | If I had had money, I would have traveled. |

**Triplet 3 — health:**

| Type | Sentence | English |
|------|----------|---------|
| Type 0 | Wenn man zu viel isst, **nimmt** man zu. | If one eats too much, one gains weight. |

### 6.12. Word Formation: Partizip I/II as Established Adjectives (10)

Many participles have become independent adjectives whose meaning is fixed — you can no longer derive them reliably from the verb. Note the pattern: Partizip I (anstrengend, überraschend, spannend) is active/ongoing; Partizip II (begeistert, enttäuscht, interessiert) is passive/completed.

| Partizip | Base Verb | Meaning | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|---------|
| anstrengend (Partizip I) | anstrengen | tiring | Der Tag war anstrengend. |
| überraschend (Partizip I) | überraschen | surprising | Das Ergebnis ist überraschend. |
| begeistert (Partizip II) | begeistern | enthusiastic | Das Publikum war begeistert. |
| enttäuscht (Partizip II) | enttäuschen | disappointed | Sie ist enttäuscht vom Film. |
| spannend (Partizip I) | spannen | exciting | Der Film ist spannend. |
| interessiert (Partizip II) | interessieren | interested | Er ist an Kunst interessiert. |
| überzeugend (Partizip I) | überzeugen | convincing | Das Argument ist überzeugend. |
| gelungen (Partizip II) | gelingen | successful, well done | Die Feier war gelungen. |
| beruhigend (Partizip I) | beruhigen | calming | Die Musik wirkt beruhigend. |
| gefordert (Partizip II) | fordern | demanding, challenged | Die Mitarbeiter sind gefordert. |

> **Note:** these adjectives are stored in the dictionary as separate entries — *interessiert* does not mean "interessed", it means "interested", and *gelungen* has drifted from gelingen (to succeed) to simply "well done". ### 8.8. Answer Key

**2.5. Choosing the Right Construction**

1. **Die Tür ist geschlossen.** (The door is closed. — Zustandspassiv: a result state, no action.)
2. **Hier spricht man Deutsch.** (German is spoken here. — man + active: casual speech, no agent.)
3. **Das Formular ist auszufüllen.** / **Das Formular muss ausgefüllt werden.** (The form is to be filled out. — sein + zu / modal passive: formal written.)
4. **Das lässt sich leicht reparieren.** (That can easily be repaired. — sich lassen: possibility, reassuring tone.)

**8.1. Vorgangspassiv vs. Zustandspassiv Identification**

1. **VP** — **Die Tür wird geöffnet.** (The door is being opened. — action in progress.)
2. **ZP** — **Die Tür ist geöffnet.** (The door is open. — result state.)
3. **ZP** — **Der Brief ist geschrieben.** (The letter is written. — result state.)
4. **VP** — **Der Brief wird gerade geschrieben.** (The letter is being written right now. — action in progress.)
5. **ZP** — **Das Fenster war geschlossen.** (The window was closed. — result state, Präteritum.)
6. **VP** — **Das Fenster wurde geschlossen.** (The window was closed. — action in the past.)
7. **ZP** — **Das Licht ist ausgeschaltet.** (The light is off. — result state.)
8. **VP** — **Das Licht wird ausgeschaltet.** (The light is being switched off. — action in progress.)
9. **ZP** — **Die Prüfung ist korrigiert.** (The exam is corrected. — result state.)
10. **VP** — **Die Prüfung wird korrigiert.** (The exam is being corrected. — action in progress.)
