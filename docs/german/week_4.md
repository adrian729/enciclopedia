## 1. Genitive Case — Possession & Attribution

The **genitive case** expresses possession and attribution ("the man's car", "the color of the leaves"). It is the fourth and final case of the German system — with Nominative, Accusative, and Dative already mastered, the genitive completes the picture.

### 1.1. Genitive Article Forms

| Article Type | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|--------------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Definite | des | der | des | der |
| Indefinite | eines | einer | eines | — |

The **der-words** (dieser, jeder, welcher — see Week 2, §2.2; alle, manche — see Week 3, §3.4) take exactly the same genitive endings as the definite article: **dieses/dieser/dieses/dieser**, **jedes/jeder** (jeder is singular only), **welches/welcher/welches/welcher**. This completes the genitive gap left open at the end of Week 3.

### 1.2. Genitive Noun Endings: -s vs. -es

**Masculine and neuter** nouns add **-(e)s** in the genitive singular. (Feminine and plural nouns never change.)

**-es** is used:
- After nouns of **one syllable**: des Mannes, des Tages, des Buches
- After nouns ending in **-s, -ß, -x, -z, -sch, -tz**: des Hauses, des Fußes, des Autobusses

**-s** (no extra e) is used after longer (polysyllabic) nouns, especially those ending in **-el, -er, -en, -chen**, and after nouns ending in an unstressed **vowel** (e.g. **-o**): des Vaters, des Lehrers, des Mädchens, des Fensters, des Autos.

Examples: des Mannes (of the man), des Autos (of the car), des Tisches (of the table), des Vaters (of the father), des Kindes (of the child), des Hauses (of the house).

### 1.3. Genitive for Possession

1. Das Auto **des Mannes** ist neu. (The man's car is new.)
2. Die Tasche **der Frau** ist rot. (The woman's bag is red.)
3. Der Name **des Kindes** ist Lena. (The child's name is Lena.)
4. Die Farbe **des Hauses** ist weiß. (The color of the house is white.)
5. Der Titel **des Buches** ist interessant. (The title of the book is interesting.)
6. Die Freunde **der Studenten** kommen aus Spanien. (The students' friends come from Spain.)

### 1.4. Genitive with Proper Names

Proper names (people, cities, countries) add a plain **-s** with **no apostrophe**:

1. **Annas** Buch ist neu. (Anna's book is new.)
2. **Toms** Auto ist blau. (Tom's car is blue.)
3. **Berlins** Geschichte ist lang. (Berlin's history is long.)
4. **Deutschlands** Hauptstadt ist Berlin. (Germany's capital is Berlin.)

**Exception:** Names already ending in **-s, -ß, -x, -z** add only an **apostrophe**, no extra -s:

5. **Thomas'** Buch ist neu. (Thomas's book is new.)
6. **Max'** Fahrrad ist kaputt. (Max's bicycle is broken.)

### 1.5. Genitive Prepositions

These prepositions always take the genitive case (in careful written and formal spoken German):

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| wegen | because of | **Wegen des Regens** bleiben wir zu Hause. (Because of the rain, we're staying home.) |
| trotz | despite | **Trotz des schlechten Wetters** gingen wir spazieren. (Despite the bad weather, we went for a walk.) |
| während | during | **Während des Urlaubs** haben wir viel gelesen. (During the vacation, we read a lot.) |
| statt / anstatt | instead of | **Statt eines Autos** kaufte sie ein Fahrrad. (Instead of a car, she bought a bicycle.) |
| innerhalb | within | **Innerhalb einer Woche** war das Projekt fertig. (Within a week, the project was finished.) |
| außerhalb | outside of | Das Haus liegt **außerhalb der Stadt**. (The house is located outside the city.) |

> **Note:** In everyday spoken German, wegen and trotz are often heard with the dative (wegen dem Regen). This is common but considered less formally correct — use the genitive in writing.

### 1.6. Complete Four-Case Article Table

| Case | Masc. (der) | Fem. (die) | Neut. (das) | Plural (die) |
|------|-------------|------------|-------------|--------------|
| Nom | der | die | das | die |
| Acc | den | die | das | die |
| Dat | dem | der | dem | den (+n) |
| Gen | des | der | des | der |

| Case | Masc. (ein) | Fem. (eine) | Neut. (ein) |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Nom | ein | eine | ein |
| Acc | einen | eine | ein |
| Dat | einem | einer | einem |
| Gen | eines | einer | eines |

| Case | Masc. (mein) | Fem. (meine) | Neut. (mein) | Plural (meine) |
|------|--------------|--------------|--------------|----------------|
| Nom | mein | meine | mein | meine |
| Acc | meinen | meine | mein | meine |
| Dat | meinem | meiner | meinem | meinen (+n) |
| Gen | meines | meiner | meines | meiner |

All possessive articles (dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr) follow the same pattern as **mein**.

### 1.7. Genitive Adjective Endings — Completing Week 3's Tables

Week 3 introduced weak, mixed, and strong adjective endings across Nominative, Accusative, and Dative — deliberately leaving out the genitive row until the case itself was taught. Here is the missing row for all three tables.

**Weak (after der-words) — Genitive row:**

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Gen | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** |

**Mixed (after ein-words) — Genitive row:**

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Gen | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** |

**Strong (no article) — Genitive row:**

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Gen | -**en** | -**er** | -**en** | -**er** |

> **Why strong Gen masc/neut is -en, not -es:** The noun itself already carries the genitive signal (des Mannes, des Kindes), so the adjective doesn't need to double-mark it — it relaxes to -en, exactly like the dative. Feminine and plural nouns never change, so the adjective must carry the full signal there (-er).

**4 worked examples (one per type, plus a mixed-context check):**

**Example 1 (weak):** "the color of the old house"
- der-word present (des) → **weak**
- Gen neut → ending: **-en**
- Result: die Farbe **des alten Hauses**

**Example 2 (mixed):** "the title of my new book"
- ein-word present (meines) → **mixed**
- meines already shows the signal → adjective relaxes
- Gen neut → **-en**
- Result: der Titel **meines neuen Buches**

**Example 3 (strong):** "the scent of fresh flowers"
- No article → **strong**
- Gen pl → **-er**
- Result: der Duft **frischer Blumen**

**Example 4 (mixed context — figure out the type):** "because of this cold weather"
- der-word present (dieses) → **weak**
- Gen neut → **-en**
- Result: wegen **dieses kalten Wetters**

## 2. Comparative & Superlative

### 2.1. Comparative Formation

The comparative is formed with stem + **-er**. Monosyllabic adjectives with a stem vowel **a, o,** or **u** usually add an **umlaut**.

**10 regular comparatives (no umlaut):**

| Adjective | Comparative | English |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| schnell | schneller | faster |
| klein | kleiner | smaller |
| billig | billiger | cheaper |
| schön | schöner | more beautiful |
| interessant | interessanter | more interesting |
| langsam | langsamer | slower |
| laut | lauter | louder |
| leise | leiser | quieter |
| wichtig | wichtiger | more important |
| neu | neuer | newer |

**5 with umlaut (monosyllabic a/o/u → ä/ö/ü):**

| Adjective | Comparative | English |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| alt | älter | older |
| groß | größer | bigger |
| jung | jünger | younger |
| lang | länger | longer |
| kurz | kürzer | shorter |

> **Note:** Adjectives ending in **-er** or **-el** drop the final **e** before adding -er: teuer → teu**r**er (not ~~teuerer~~), dunkel → dunk**l**er.

### 2.2. Superlative Formation

Two forms: **am** + stem + **-sten** (predicate: "Berlin ist am größten") or **der/die/das** + stem + **-ste** (attributive: "die größte Stadt"). Stems ending in **-d, -t, -s, -ß, -z, -sch**, or a **vowel**, add **-esten** for pronunciation.

**10 examples:**

| Adjective | Superlative (am ___) | Notes |
|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| schnell | am schnellsten | regular -sten |
| klein | am kleinsten | regular -sten |
| neu | am neuesten | ends in vowel → -esten |
| alt | am ältesten | ends in -t → -esten, + umlaut |
| kalt | am kältesten | ends in -t → -esten, + umlaut |
| heiß | am heißesten | ends in -ß → -esten |
| groß | am größten | **exception:** ends in -ß but no extra -e-, despite the -ß rule |
| laut | am lautesten | ends in -t → -esten |
| frisch | am frischesten | ends in -sch → -esten |
| wichtig | am wichtigsten | regular -sten |

> **Exception to flag:** groß is the one common -ß adjective that does **not** take -esten (am größten, not ~~am größesten~~). Compare heiß, which does take it regularly (am heißesten).

### 2.3. Irregular Comparative & Superlative Forms

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative | English |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| gut | besser | am besten | good/well |
| viel | mehr | am meisten | much/most |
| gern | lieber | am liebsten | gladly (like to do) |
| hoch | höher | am höchsten | high |
| nah | näher | am nächsten | near |

> **hoch** drops its final **-ch** before the comparative ending (hoch → höh + er = höher) but keeps it in the superlative (am höch + sten = am höchsten). The same **hoch → hoh-** shortening happens whenever hoch takes a plain adjective ending before a noun, even in the positive (uncompared) form: ein **hohes** Haus, die **hohe** Miete (not ~~hoches~~/~~hoche~~) — only the bare predicate form stays "hoch" (Das Haus ist hoch).

### 2.4. Comparison Structures: als, so...wie, je...desto

**als** (than — used with the comparative, for inequality):
1. Berlin ist **größer als** München. (Berlin is bigger than Munich.)
2. Sie ist **klüger als** ihr Bruder. (She is smarter than her brother.)
3. Das Auto ist **teurer als** das Fahrrad. (The car is more expensive than the bicycle.)

**so...wie** (as...as — for equality; negated for inequality without a comparative):
1. Berlin ist **nicht so groß wie** London. (Berlin is not as big as London.)
2. Er ist **so alt wie** ich. (He is as old as I am.)
3. Das Buch ist **so interessant wie** der Film. (The book is as interesting as the film.)

**je...desto** (the...the — correlative comparative; verb goes to the end of the je-clause, and the desto-clause has V2 with desto in position 1):
1. **Je mehr** ich übe, **desto besser** spreche ich Deutsch. (The more I practice, the better I speak German.)
2. **Je älter** das Haus ist, **desto interessanter** finde ich es. (The older the house is, the more interesting I find it.)
3. **Je schneller** wir fahren, **desto früher** kommen wir an. (The faster we drive, the earlier we arrive.) — *ankommen is separable (see Week 5)*

### 2.5. Comparative & Superlative Before Nouns

When a comparative or superlative comes before a noun, it takes normal adjective endings (weak/mixed/strong, from Week 3 and §1.7 above) — the comparative/superlative form is simply treated as the adjective stem.

1. **der ältere** Mann (the older man) — weak Nom masc -e
2. **eine kleinere** Wohnung (a smaller apartment) — mixed Nom/Acc fem -e
3. **das teuerste** Auto (the most expensive car) — weak Nom/Acc neut -e
4. mit **dem besten** Freund (with the best friend) — weak Dat masc -en
5. **größere** Städte (bigger cities) — strong Nom/Acc pl -e
6. die Hauptstadt **des größeren** Landes (the capital of the bigger country) — weak Gen neut -en

## 3. N-Deklination (Weak Masculine Nouns)

### 3.1. What Is N-Declension?

A small but frequent group of **masculine** nouns — mostly referring to people (and a few animals) — add **-(e)n** in every case **except** the nominative singular. The nominative stays bare; every other slot (Acc, Dat, Gen singular, and the entire plural) takes the ending.

### 3.2. Full Declension Pattern

**der Junge (the boy) — regular pattern:**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nom | der Junge | die Jungen |
| Acc | den Jungen | die Jungen |
| Dat | dem Jungen | den Jungen |
| Gen | des Jungen | der Jungen |

**der Herr (special: only -n, not -en, in the singular):**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nom | der Herr | die Herren |
| Acc | den Herrn | die Herren |
| Dat | dem Herrn | den Herren |
| Gen | des Herrn | der Herren |

**das Herz (irregular — neuter, and the only case without an ending is the accusative, not just the nominative):**

| Case | Singular |
|------|----------|
| Nom | das Herz |
| Acc | das Herz |
| Dat | dem Herzen |
| Gen | des Herzens |

> **das Herz is doubly irregular:** it's the only common **neuter** noun that behaves like an N-declension noun, and unlike every masculine N-noun, its accusative stays bare (das Herz, not ~~den Herzen~~) — only dative and genitive take the ending, and the genitive adds an extra -s (Herzens, not ~~Herzen~~).

### 3.3. 15 Common N-Declension Nouns

1. der Junge — boy
2. der Herr [Herrn] — gentleman, Mr.
3. der Mensch — human being, person
4. der Student — (university) student
5. der Nachbar [Nachbarn] — neighbor
6. der Kollege — colleague
7. der Kunde — customer
8. der Polizist — police officer
9. der Tourist — tourist
10. der Journalist — journalist
11. der Architekt — architect
12. der Affe — monkey, ape
13. der Löwe — lion
14. der Name — name
15. das Herz [irregular: des Herzens] — heart

> **Note on der Name:** like das Herz, der Name has an irregular genitive with an extra -s: **des Namens** (not ~~des Namen~~). Acc/Dat are regular: den Namen, dem Namen.

> **Note on der Nachbar:** like der Herr, der Nachbar takes only **-n** (not the "regular" -en) in every oblique case, singular and plural: den/dem/des **Nachbarn**, die **Nachbarn** (not ~~Nachbaren~~).

Examples using the case: Ich kenne **den Studenten**. (Acc) Ich helfe **dem Kollegen**. (Dat) Das ist das Auto **des Nachbarn**. (Gen) Die Mähne **des Löwen** ist braun. (Gen)

### 3.4. Recognition Patterns

Two broad clues help you spot an N-declension noun:

1. **Nouns ending in -e that refer to a living being:** der Junge, der Kollege, der Kunde, der Affe, der Löwe (and der Name, for things — the pattern is about the -e ending, not just living beings).
2. **International/loan-word professions and roles with a stressed final syllable**, typically ending in **-ent, -ant, -ist, -at, -ekt, -oge, -graf**: der Student (-ent), der Polizist / der Tourist / der Journalist (-ist), der Architekt (-ekt). (Other common examples outside our core 15: der Diplomat [-at], der Biologe [-oge], der Fotograf [-graf].)

> **A small "-n only" exception class:** der Herr and der Nachbar don't fit either pattern above, and both take just **-n** instead of the expected **-en** in the oblique cases (den Herrn/Nachbarn, not ~~Herren/Nachbaren~~ in the singular). There's no larger rule to derive this from — just memorize these two alongside the 15 core nouns.

> **A few don't fit any pattern at all:** der Mensch is a common, regular N-noun (den/dem/des Menschen) but matches neither clue above — it doesn't end in -e, and it isn't a stressed loan-word suffix. Some N-declension nouns simply have to be memorized individually rather than recognized by shape.

## 5. Applied Skills

### 5.1. Reading: Comparing Two Cities

Read the following text and answer the questions below.

---

**München und Hamburg**

München und Hamburg sind zwei der größten Städte Deutschlands, aber sie sind sehr verschieden. München liegt im Süden, nicht weit von den Alpen. Hamburg liegt im Norden, näher an der Nordsee. Wegen seiner Lage im Süden ist das Wetter in München oft kälter im Winter, aber im Sommer genauso warm wie in Hamburg.

Hamburg ist wegen seines großen Hafens bekannt — er ist einer der größten Häfen Europas. München ist bekannt für sein Oktoberfest und die Nähe der Berge. Beide Städte haben auch viele Museen, Theater und Sehenswürdigkeiten.

Die Mieten in München sind höher als die Mieten in Hamburg — München ist die teuerste Stadt Deutschlands. Hamburg ist etwas günstiger, aber immer noch teuer. Beide Städte sind groß, aber Berlin bleibt die größte Stadt Deutschlands.

Für viele Touristen ist diese Entscheidung schwierig. Welche Stadt ist schöner? Am Ende ist das einfach eine Frage des Geschmacks.

> **New words in this text:** der Süden (south), der Norden (north), die Nordsee (North Sea), die Lage (location), weit (far), der Hafen / die Häfen (harbor/harbors), die Nähe (proximity), beide (both), die Entscheidung (decision), die Frage (question), das Ende (end), einfach (simply, just).

---

**Comprehension Questions** (answer in German):

1. Wo liegt München und wo liegt Hamburg?
2. Warum ist Hamburg bekannt?
3. Warum ist München bekannt?
4. Welche Stadt hat höhere Mieten?
